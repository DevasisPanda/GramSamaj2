Earth Day and Environmental Awareness Activities
Earth Day marked the first community awareness activity organized by the Self-Help Groups (SHGs) of Village Garhi under the guidance of AIRD. The objective of the programme was to help villagers understand the importance of the Earth and recognize their collective responsibility in conserving natural resources and maintaining environmental balance for sustainable development.
To support the initiative, AIRD provided educational literature highlighting environmental challenges, the importance of natural resources, and the role of local communities in protecting the environment. Stationery materials were also provided to SHG members for organizing a Slogan Writing Competition on Environmental Conservation.
The competition was designed not only to encourage creativity but also to promote family participation in environmental education. Participants were given adequate time and encouraged to seek support from family members in composing meaningful slogans. This approach helped extend discussions on environmental issues beyond SHG meetings and into households across the village.
The Earth Day activity was followed by a meeting of SHG members on World Environment Day, during which the slogans submitted by participants were reviewed and discussed. The members selected a number of slogans that effectively conveyed messages related to environmental conservation, sustainable resource management, and community responsibility.
It was decided that these slogans would be used in future awareness campaigns, village meetings, educational programmes, and community events to encourage villagers to adopt environmentally responsible practices and actively participate in protecting their local ecosystem.
The activity demonstrated the potential of SHGs as effective platforms for promoting environmental awareness and mobilizing community action for sustainable development. Through simple participatory activities, AIRD was able to initiate dialogue on environmental issues and strengthen the involvement of women and families in environmental stewardship.

Environmental awareness through children and self-help groups
The environmental awareness campaign initiated on Earth Day was further strengthened by involving village children in creative activities. The selected environmental slogans were provided to children for preparing placards and posters. Members of the Self-Help Groups (SHGs) viewed this activity as an opportunity to encourage creativity among their children while simultaneously spreading awareness about environmental conservation within the community.
Independence Day Celebration
On 15 August 2020, the SHGs of Village Garhi celebrated India's Independence Day with enthusiasm and community participation. A village rally was organized in which children and women carried placards displaying environmental and development-related slogans prepared during the awareness campaign.
During the programme, Mr. Jitendra Shukla, former Gram Pradhan and Trustee of AIRD, felicitated women and children who had participated in poster-making, slogan writing, and artistic activities. The event encouraged community members to recognize the importance of active citizenship and collective responsibility.
Following the rally, an open meeting of villagers was organized. Participants collectively took an oath to continue working towards the establishment of Gram Swaraj in Village Garhi and resolved to persevere until a practical and demonstrable model of participatory governance was established.
Celebration of Service and Compassion
On 25 September 2020, the birthday of Mr. Christopher Baron, a Barrister based in London and a long-time supporter of community development initiatives in India, was celebrated in Village Garhi.
Mr. Baron has consistently advocated that serving resource-poor communities is one of the most meaningful paths to personal happiness and social well-being. He had earlier supported participatory action research initiatives aimed at developing practical tools for strengthening collective action and helping communities address their financial and social needs.
Many years earlier, students of the Master of Social Work programme at Lucknow University had affectionately named him “Bank Uncle” during their fieldwork with the Appropriate Technology Development Association (ATDA), Gandhi Bhawan, Lucknow, in recognition of his support for community-based development efforts.
During the celebration, SHG members appreciated the philosophy of mutual support and resolved to strengthen cooperation within the community by assisting one another during times of need.
Review of expansion efforts in Kannauj
On 29 November 2020, a meeting was organized with Block Resource Persons (BRPs) in Village Akbarpur, District Kannauj, to explore the possibility of developing the village as a model of participatory development. However, the response from local residents was not sufficiently encouraging. In addition, the geographical distance from AIRD's operational base created practical challenges for sustained engagement.
Based on these experiences, it was concluded that efforts would be concentrated in Village Garhi, where stronger community participation and institutional support had already emerged.
First foundation day of AIRD
The First Foundation Day of AIRD was celebrated on 3 December 2020 in the lawns of Ramakrishna Math, Lucknow. Members reflected on the progress achieved during the first year of the organization and expressed satisfaction with the outcomes of their collective efforts despite limited resources.
After reviewing the experiences gained from different villages, it was unanimously decided that AIRD would focus its efforts exclusively on Village Garhi until a successful and replicable model of participatory rural development was established. Members recognized that progress might be gradual, but they remained confident that a genuine demonstration of Gram Swaraj could only be achieved through patience, persistence, and community ownership.
National Youth Day and reaffirmation of commitment
On 12 January 2021, AIRD members participated in a meditation programme at Ramakrishna Math to commemorate the birth anniversary of Swami Vivekananda. The programme included devotional songs and reflections on Vivekananda's teachings related to service, self-development, and nation-building.
Inspired by the occasion, members reaffirmed their commitment to continue working until the vision of Gram Swaraj was successfully demonstrated in Village Garhi.
Board of Trustees Meeting
The first Board of Trustees meeting after registration was held on 30 December 2020. The Board reviewed the initiatives undertaken in Village Garhi and appreciated the dedication of community members and volunteers.
The Trustees observed that one of the greatest challenges was convincing villagers that AIRD was working for community development without external funding and without pursuing personal gain. Building trust and confidence among villagers was recognized as a gradual process requiring consistent engagement and transparency.
The Board expressed disappointment that the initiative in Kannauj had not progressed as expected. Consequently, it resolved to concentrate organizational resources and efforts within Lucknow District and replicate the model elsewhere only after establishing a successful demonstration village in Garhi.
Members also welcomed the fact that AIRD had successfully opened its State Bank of India account on 30 June 2020, strengthening the organization's financial management system. Efforts were also initiated to identify local individuals who could assist in operating a village-level activity account in Malihabad to facilitate future community initiatives.
The meeting further finalized:
The core teachings and values to be promoted through AIRD's programmes. 
The overall strategy for achieving the organization's vision of Gram Swaraj. 
The conceptual framework for future participatory development interventions. 
A concept note to serve as the foundation for developing project proposals and resource mobilization efforts. 
The meeting concluded with a renewed commitment to establishing a practical and sustainable model of participatory rural development that could eventually be replicated in other villages across India.
Philosophy of AIRD
The philosophy of AIRD is founded on the belief that humanity, service, and spiritual growth are inseparable components of sustainable development. While religions may differ in their names, traditions, and practices, the fundamental message conveyed by all great faiths is remarkably similar: serve the needy, practice compassion, and uphold humanity.
AIRD believes that there is one universal and invisible power that governs creation, operation, and transformation in the universe. Different religions describe this power in different ways, yet all seek to guide human beings toward truth, wisdom, and inner peace. Understanding this universal reality requires an open mind, self-reflection, and a willingness to learn beyond inherited beliefs and social divisions.
Service as a path to wisdom
According to AIRD's philosophy, selfless service to those in need is one of the highest forms of spiritual practice. The blessings earned through genuine acts of kindness and compassion enrich both the individual and society. Such blessings help people develop wisdom, strengthen their character, and better understand their purpose in life.
The organization encourages individuals to engage in community service, promote social harmony, and contribute to the welfare of others without expectation of personal gain.
The concept of Development Car (DC)
AIRD uses the concept of the Development Car (DC) as a symbolic tool for understanding human life and encouraging positive action.
According to this concept, every human being is a combination of:
A visible body, which exists in the physical world; and 
An invisible soul, which represents consciousness and spiritual existence. 
The body is temporary, while the soul continues its journey beyond physical life. The Development Car symbolizes the spiritual vehicle through which the soul travels during its journey through existence.
The quality of this journey is influenced by the thoughts, actions, and values practiced during life. Positive actions, compassion, honesty, and service strengthen this symbolic vehicle, while selfishness, injustice, and harm to others weaken it.
The concept is intended to inspire people to live responsibly, treat others with respect, and strive to leave a positive impact on society.
The importance of thoughts
AIRD recognizes the power of human thought in shaping individual lives and collective development. Inspired by the teachings of great spiritual leaders, including Swami Vivekananda, the organization promotes the idea that thoughts influence actions, actions shape character, and character determines destiny.
Positive thinking, self-discipline, meditation, and continuous learning are therefore considered essential for personal growth and community leadership.
Three dimensions of life
The AIRD philosophy views life as interconnected across different dimensions of existence:
The physical world, where people live, work, and interact. 
The developmental stage of life, where human existence begins and grows. 
The spiritual dimension, which connects humanity with the larger universe and the source of life. 
These dimensions are interdependent and remind human beings of their responsibility to maintain harmony with nature, society, and their inner selves.
Towards holistic development
AIRD promotes a model of development that combines:
Spiritual growth 
Ethical leadership 
Social responsibility 
Environmental sustainability 
Community participation 
Economic self-reliance 
The organization believes that lasting development can only be achieved when material progress is accompanied by moral values, compassion, and collective responsibility.
Core message
Serve humanity. Protect nature. Strengthen communities. Practice meditation and self-reflection. Earn blessings through service rather than recognition through power. Improve your thoughts, improve your actions, and contribute to a society where every individual can live with dignity, harmony, and purpose.
This philosophy forms the foundation of AIRD's efforts to promote participatory development and realize the vision of Gram Swaraj through collective action and community empowerment.
Strategy for Achieving Gram Swaraj
AIRD adopted a participatory and collaborative strategy for demonstrating Gram Swaraj in Village Garhi. The strategy recognizes that sustainable rural development can be achieved only when individuals, community institutions, government agencies, academic institutions, and development organizations work together towards a common goal.
Building a foundation of values and service
AIRD seeks to bring together individuals and institutions willing to “Join Hands and Take Action” for establishing an appropriate community learning and development centre in Village Garhi. Inspired by the teachings of Swami Vivekananda and Gautama Buddha, the initiative promotes spiritual science, meditation, self-development, and service to humanity.
The organization encourages people to view service to the needy as a form of worship and to contribute their time, knowledge, and resources for the welfare of society. Students and young professionals are invited to participate as volunteers and gain practical experience in community development while contributing to the establishment of a live model of participatory governance.
Promoting multi-stakeholder partnerships
The strategy aims to create a common platform where key stakeholders of rural development—including Self-Help Groups (SHGs), Gram Panchayats, Government Departments, Banks, NGOs, Corporate Institutions, Universities, and Research Organizations—can work together to achieve the shared objective of community empowerment and people’s governance.
Universities and research institutions are encouraged to demonstrate and validate their innovations, technologies, and development models in the village. Particular emphasis is placed on approaches that strengthen community participation, improve agricultural productivity, enhance livestock-based livelihoods, promote environmental conservation, and improve public health outcomes.
Developing local Change Agents
Recognizing the importance of local leadership, AIRD seeks to identify and train rural youth as Change Agents (CAs). Practical training programmes are organized on participatory planning, implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and social accountability.
Self-Help Groups are encouraged to identify motivated young individuals who are willing to invest their time, energy, and commitment in community development. These youth are trained and mentored to facilitate development processes within their own villages, thereby ensuring local ownership and sustainability.
Participatory planning and project development
AIRD supports community members in identifying local needs and opportunities through participatory discussions and collective decision-making. The organization facilitates the preparation of development projects and helps communities establish partnerships with government agencies, financial institutions, and corporate organizations for resource mobilization and implementation.
Special attention is given to strengthening community participation in flagship programmes such as:
MGNREGA 
National Rural Livelihood Mission (NRLM) 
Panchayati Raj initiatives 
Rural livelihood and environmental programmes 
Job card holders are encouraged to plan effectively for wage employment opportunities under MGNREGA and participate actively in social audits. Similarly, SHGs and their federations are supported in preparing Micro Investment Plans (MIPs) and Micro Credit Plans (MCPs) to strengthen household livelihoods and financial inclusion.
Strengthening Gram Sabha and local governance
A central component of the strategy is strengthening the Gram Sabha as the foundation of democratic local governance. AIRD facilitates community participation in Gram Sabha meetings and supports the systematic documentation of discussions, resolutions, and development priorities.
The objective is to build the capacity of villagers to participate effectively in planning, monitoring, and evaluating local development interventions and to ensure transparency and accountability in the management of public resources.
Community dialogue and Social Accountability
To encourage continuous dialogue among villagers, AIRD promotes the organization of Maitri Bhoj (Friendship Dinners) on every Full Moon (Purnima) and New Moon (Amavasya). These informal gatherings provide opportunities for community members to discuss issues related to service delivery, resource management, village development, and social harmony.
The organization also promotes electronic documentation of community feedback regarding the performance of local institutions such as Gram Panchayats, SHGs, government functionaries, and development programmes. These experiences are intended to be documented and shared through digital platforms to enhance transparency and public accountability.
Village information and knowledge management
AIRD seeks to establish a Village Information Bank for systematically collecting, documenting, and monitoring information related to village resources, development initiatives, service delivery systems, and community priorities.
The information generated through participatory processes is intended to support evidence-based planning and decision-making. Village development plans and community initiatives are to be shared through digital platforms, inviting individuals and institutions to collaborate in achieving common development goals.
Learning, reflection, and process improvement
At the completion of the demonstration phase, AIRD proposes to organize a workshop on participatory rural development to evaluate the effectiveness of the processes adopted. The workshop will review achievements, challenges, lessons learned, and innovations generated during implementation.
The ultimate objective is to refine and demonstrate a practical, replicable, and community-owned model of Gram Swaraj that strengthens local institutions, promotes transparency and accountability, enhances livelihoods, and empowers people to take collective responsibility for their own development.
Through this strategy, AIRD aims not merely to implement projects but to create a living demonstration of people-centred governance, participatory development, and sustainable rural transformation.
Concept and Rationale
The quality of rural development largely depends on two critical factors: the effectiveness of service delivery systems and the management of local resources. When these systems are transparent, accountable, and participatory, they can significantly improve the quality of life of rural communities and accelerate the development of the nation.
Over the years, development practitioners, researchers, and policymakers have evolved several participatory approaches to strengthen local governance, improve service delivery, and ensure community ownership of development initiatives. These approaches have been widely discussed and validated through research studies, workshops, and conferences. However, despite the availability of proven methodologies, active and meaningful participation of rural people in planning, implementation, monitoring, and social audit processes remains limited in many villages of Uttar Pradesh.
In many cases, village-level meetings organized by government departments, banks, corporate institutions, and development agencies witness low attendance and minimal engagement from community members. As a result, development interventions often fail to fully reflect local priorities and aspirations.
One of the major reasons for this situation is the erosion of public trust caused by a lack of transparency and accountability in the identification of beneficiaries and distribution of development benefits. Although people living Below Poverty Line (BPL) are intended to be the primary beneficiaries of many government programmes, inaccuracies in beneficiary selection have often resulted in exclusion of deserving families while some relatively better-off households continue to receive benefits meant for the poor.
Consequently, many villagers question the value of participating in community meetings and development activities, often asking, “What benefit will participation bring to us?” This mindset poses a significant challenge to participatory governance and collective action.
Opportunities created by NRLM
The Government of India has introduced the National Rural Livelihood Mission (NRLM) as a major initiative to address rural poverty through community institutions. NRLM promotes the identification of the Poorest of the Poor (PoP) through a Participatory Identification Process (PIP) conducted at the village level.
Women from identified households are organized into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and further strengthened through federations at village, cluster, and block levels. These institutions provide a platform for savings, credit access, livelihood promotion, social empowerment, and collective action.
NRLM also supports the preparation of:
Micro Investment Plans (MIPs) for individual households. 
Micro Credit Plans (MCPs) for Self-Help Groups. 
These tools enable poor families, especially women, to plan and manage their economic activities more effectively and gradually overcome poverty and dependence on informal moneylenders.
Opportunities created by MGNREGA and Social Audit
The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) provides a legal guarantee of up to 100 days of wage employment to eligible rural households. Beyond employment generation, the programme contributes to the creation and strengthening of community assets and natural resource infrastructure that support sustainable livelihoods.
To enhance transparency, accountability, and community participation, Social Audit has been made a mandatory component of MGNREGA and several other rural development programmes, including the Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana (PMAY). Social Audit provides a mechanism through which citizens can review development interventions, raise concerns, and ensure that public resources are utilized effectively and fairly.
In addition, the increasing use of digital platforms and Management Information Systems (MIS) has improved transparency in planning, financial transactions, implementation, and monitoring of government programmes.
Need for a demonstration village
Despite the existence of these progressive initiatives, there remains a need to demonstrate how various programmes, institutions, and participatory processes can work together effectively at the village level.
AIRD believes that the most practical way to strengthen community participation is to establish a live demonstration village where local people, Self-Help Groups, Gram Panchayats, government agencies, banks, academic institutions, and development organizations work collectively to address local issues and improve governance.
The proposed model seeks to demonstrate how:
Gram Sabha participation can be strengthened. 
SHGs can become agents of social and economic change. 
MGNREGA and NRLM can complement each other. 
Social Audit can enhance transparency and accountability. 
Local resources can be managed more effectively. 
Community institutions can collaborate for sustainable development. 
Digital technologies can improve access to information and public oversight. 
Digital transparency and knowledge sharing
To support transparency, documentation, and wider learning, the Board of Trustees resolved to develop an official AIRD website. The website would serve as a platform for sharing information about organizational activities, village development initiatives, research findings, community experiences, and opportunities for collaboration.
The objective is to enable individuals, institutions, researchers, students, donors, and development practitioners to observe the progress of the initiative, learn from its experiences, and participate in the collective effort of building a practical model of Gram Swaraj based on participation, transparency, accountability, and sustainable development.
Through this approach, AIRD aims to demonstrate that meaningful rural transformation is possible when communities are empowered to become active partners in planning, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating their own development.
