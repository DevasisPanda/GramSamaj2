Key to Reform & Adopt Noble Treatment Initiatives (KRANTI) 
1.0 Introduction: 
India has a long tradition of democratic institutions at the village level. References in the Rigveda to assemblies such as Sabha and Samiti indicate that collective decision-making processes existed in rural society since ancient times. After Independence, several reforms were undertaken to strengthen the Panchayati Raj system and promote local self-governance.
The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, implemented in 1993, granted constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions and established the Gram Sabha as the foundation of grassroots democracy. Subsequently, various government programmes such as Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA), Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana - National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM) , and the Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP) were introduced to encourage community participation in rural development. Moreover, the community has been empowered for bringing transparency and establishes accountability by conducting Social Audit in an open meeting of Gram Sabha. 
However, despite these institutional arrangements, in many places participation in Gram Sabha meetings and social audit remains low, coordination among local institutions is weak, and awareness about development schemes is limited.
During a Participatory Action Research conducted in and around village Bari Garhi of Malihabad block in Lucknow, the Appropriate Institute of Rural Development (AIRD) developed a participatory approach aimed at mobilizing communities for collective development. In order to demonstrate this approach in practice, project KRANTI is being proposed to develop a model village based on participatory governance and effective management of local resources.
The objective of Project KRANTI is to establish partnerships among local communities, government institutions, banks, and corporate organizations. Development practitioners, volunteers, and institutions interested in rural transformation will also be invited to collaborate in this initiative.
Through this effort, AIRD aims to establish a living model village where community participation, transparency, and accountability become central elements of the development process. The Project KRANTI seeks to demonstrate a participatory development process in which the community itself plans, implements, and monitors its development activities by developing a digitally enabled and self-reliant village in Lucknow region of Uttar Pradesh.
2.0 Aim and Objectives:
AIM of KRANTI is to develop a village as a live model that demonstrates process of strengthening people’s governance and participatory development not only in theory but through real community participation and action at the village level.
To achieve the aim objectives are mentioned below-
Promote awareness of the spiritual and ethical dimensions of development, encouraging values such as cooperation, responsibility, and community welfare.
Organize practical training programs on participatory process of rural development, with the aim of developing rural youth as Change Agents (CAs) who can facilitate community-led development processes.
Develop a team of trained CAs capable of conducting participatory studies, action research, and community mobilization for village development.
Document the functioning of Gram Panchayat, Self-Help Group (SHGs) and their Village Level Organizations (VLOs) and make this information publicly available through a digital platform by developing a village website.
Demonstrate effective processes for strengthening community participation in identifying local issues, preparing appropriate development projects and implementing them in partnership with the government institutions, banks, and corporate organizations.
Present and disseminate the findings and outcomes of village-level interventions through community meetings and digital platforms to promote transparency, learning, and replication of successful practices.
This project ultimately aims to strengthen participatory governance, improve local resource management, and promote sustainable rural development, thereby contributing to the realization of Mahatma Gandhi’s vision of Gram Swaraj.
3.0 Components: 
The project KRANTI is consisting of following key components to promote participatory governance and strengthen the process of Gram Swaraj at the village level:
1. Spiritual awareness and community mobilization: Spiritual camps will be organized in the village to promote the idea that serving the community is a form of worship. These camps will encourage values such as cooperation, mutual support, ethical leadership, and social responsibility, creating a strong moral foundation for collective development.
2. Capacity building of rural youth: Local youth will receive practical training on participatory rural development methods to develop them as Change Agents (CAs). These trained youth will also help establish an e-information bank in the village to collect and share important information related to government schemes, development programs, and local resources.
3. Participatory study and documentation on working of local institutions: The project will document the functioning of key village-level institutions such as:
Gram Sabha
Committees of the Gram Panchayat
Women Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and their Village Level Organizations (VLOs)
This documentation will help understand strengths, challenges, and opportunities for strengthening local governance.
4. Demonstration of people’s governance in Practice: A collective effort will be made to demonstrate the process of strengthening people’s governance through active participation of villagers. Local issues will be identified, and appropriate solutions will be developed and implemented through collaboration with government agencies, banks, and corporate partners. A team of trained local people will be developed to replicate the process in other villages.
5. Dissemination and Learning: 
The findings and experiences from the project will be presented through:
Open village meetings involving community members and government officials to encourage dialogue and accountability.
Workshops in the model village, where experts, planners, and development practitioners will evaluate the KRANTI process and provide guidance for replicating successful practices in nearby villages.
Through these components, the project aims to create a practical model of participatory governance and sustainable village development, which can inspire and guide similar initiatives in other rural areas.
		
		
		
		
		4.0 Activities:
The project will be implemented through a series of participatory and capacity-building activities aimed at strengthening grassroots governance and community participation in village development.
4.1 Practical Training for Local Youth: A team of local youth will be trained as Change Agents (CAs) to facilitate participatory development processes in the village. The training will include practical exercises such as preparing resource maps, social maps, and problem maps to understand village conditions and resources. The youth will collect village-level information and present it in the Gram Panchayat office for public access and discussion.
They will also establish an e-information bank in the village and link it with a website to ensure transparency and easy access to information about development programs. Through training in participatory processes and group dynamics, the Change Agents will work to bring together the Gram Panchayat, Self-Help Groups (SHGs), and community members for collective decision-making and development action.
The trained youth will further conduct awareness campaigns to inform villagers about various government development and welfare schemes, plans, and programs. Special efforts will be made to build a supportive team of men who can assist and strengthen the work of women’s Self-Help Groups as important village-level institutions.
To promote social harmony and collective reflection Maitri Bhoj (community dinners prepared by participants themselves) will be organized on every full moon night (Pooranmasi) and no moon night (Amawasya). These gatherings will also provide an opportunity to document community feedback on the functioning of the Gram Panchayat, SHGs, and village institutions, which will be recorded electronically and presented in website.
4.2 Participatory Action Study (PAS): A PAS will be conducted to document the functioning and effectiveness of local institutions and development programs of the Government. This study will examine the role of the Gram Sabha in ensuring service delivery and managing local resources, as well as the performance of various committees of the Gram Panchayat in fulfilling their responsibilities.
The study will also document the achievements of Self-Help Groups (SHGs) in empowering women, improving household livelihoods, and contributing to village development. It will assess the role of MGNREGA in providing employment to rural labors and examine the impact of social audits in promoting transparency and accountability in development programs.
In addition, the project will document the success stories of the Uttar Pradesh State Rural Livelihood Mission (UPSRLM) in empowering PoP (Poorest of the Poor) households. These real-life experiences will be compiled to develop motivational and educational materials for wider dissemination.
4.3 Participatory and Collaborative Action for Gram Swaraj: A collective and collaborative effort will be undertaken to demonstrate the practical process of strengthening people’s governance “Gram Swaraj” in the selected model village. The initiative will begin with a rally, launching of KRANTI, organized with the support of SHGs and their institutions. 
A Project Implementation Team (PIT), consisting of representatives from Self-Help Groups and the Gram Panchayat, will be formed to support the activities of the Change Agents. 
The community members, all adults above 18 years of age, will collectively identify one key local issue that requires immediate attention and action. Based on this issue, the community will be organized into working groups to address the problem by making collective action. These groups will interact with relevant government departments and officials to explore possible solutions and support. The discussions and decisions taken during these interface meetings will be documented and shared through the village website.
Regular community dinners on Pooranmasi and Amawasya will continue as platforms for reviewing progress, sharing experiences, and recording community reactions to the KRANTI process. These updates will be documented monthly and made available on the website to ensure transparency and wider learning.
4.4 Evaluation and Knowledge Sharing: At the end of the project period, a workshop will be organized in the model village to evaluate the overall impact of the project. Experts, planners, government officials, and development practitioners will be invited to review the participatory processes adopted under project KRANTI.
The workshop will identify best practices and lessons learned from the participatory process and develop recommendations for replicating best practices in surrounding villages.
Through these activities, the project aims to create a practical demonstration of participatory governance, community empowerment, and sustainable rural development, contributing to the realization of Gram Swaraj at the village level.
5.0 Methodology 
The implementation of Project KRANTI will follow a participatory, community-driven, and action-oriented methodology developed by the AIRD. The approach integrates spiritual motivation, capacity building, participatory research, digital transparency, and collective action to strengthen people’s governance (Gram Swaraj) at the village level. The project will be implemented under the guidance of the Deen Dayal Updhayay—State Institute of Rural Development. The RIRD, Lucknow will be our guide for implementing KRANTI and establishing first live model on process of community empowerment in a village of Lucknow.
5.1 Selection and adoption of village for live model: A village, based on acceptance of concept by the community, will be identified and adopted for intensive intervention. This village will serve as a live demonstration site where the KRANTI process of participatory governance, transparency, and accountability will be implemented and showcased.
5.2 Acceptance building: The project will begin with efforts to build trust and acceptance within the community through spiritual awareness camps. Teachings of Swami Vivekanand and Gautam Buddh will be used to inspire values such as self-awareness, compassion, cooperation, and service to society. Villagers will be encouraged to practice meditation for mental peace and for viewing service to others as a form of worship, thereby creating a positive environment for collective development.
5.3 Identification of youth and capacity building: With the help of SHGs and Gram Panchayat youth will be identified from every ward and developed as a Change Agent (CA). These youth will receive hands-on training from subject experts within their own village. This approach ensures the creation of a local cadre of trained youth capable of sustaining development efforts. Training will focus on participatory management of local resources and services, understanding government schemes, service delivery systems, community mobilization and leadership skills. 
5.4 Participatory field-based learning: The project will adopt a learning-by-doing approach, where Change Agents and community members will work together with experts to prepare social maps, resource maps, and problem maps, facilitate Gram Sabha meetings and promote participatory planning and decision-making. This will strengthen practical understanding and ownership among community members. 
5.5 Participatory action study (PAS): Under the guidance and support of subject experts a PAS will be conducted by CAs with active involvement of villagers to collect and analyze information on functioning of Gram Panchayat and Gram Sabha, utilization of local resources, Government service delivery systems, community needs, priority issues, working of SHGs and their institutions. This process will ensure that data is accurate, inclusive, and community-owned. It will be followed by formation and implementation of action plan in partnership of individuals and institutions. 
5.6 Digital documentation and transparency: All collected information and progress will be documented and presented through a village-level website, including status of local resources, functioning of village institutions, implementation of government schemes and planning and implementation of projects to address local specific issues. This will establish real-time transparency and accountability in governance and development processes. 
5.7 Community mobilization and collective action: The project will promote active participation of the community by encouraging regular Gram Sabha meetings, collective identification of local issues, participatory planning, monitoring, and conducting social audits.  Thus the community will be empowered to take ownership of development initiatives.  
5.8 Maitri Bhoj: Maitri Bhoj (community dinner) as a Participatory Tool will be organized on every Pooranmasi (full moon) and Amavasya (new moon) in the adopted village. These two gathering every month will serve as platforms for discussing development interventions under KRANTI, sharing community feedback and experiences, reviewing progress of village-level activities. The discussions will be recorded electronically and regularly uploaded on the village website. 
5.9 Replication of best practices: A Non-Profit Organization (NPO) at the district/block level will be identified and provided guidance for establishing a live model in their project area. Our partner NPO will develop a website of their adopted village. The website will demonstrate the process which is in field for strengthening people’s governance, inviting other institutions and stakeholders to collaborate. The NPO will facilitate project implementation and community processes, maintain digital records and website updates, present the status of local resources and governance systems.  
5.10 Formulation of KRANTI phase II: After one year of intensive implementation a workshop will be organized in model village where the Government officials, development planners will evaluate impact and formulate project for replicating success of model village. The model will then be replicated by other NPOs at district/block level, creating a network of villages practicing participatory governance.
The methodology of Project KRANTI is designed to combine spiritual motivation, participatory learning, digital transparency, and collective action. By building local capacity and strengthening institutions, the project aims to create a sustainable and replicable model of Gram Swaraj in rural Uttar Pradesh.
6.0 Presentation:
The impact and progress of Project KRANTI will be presented through a series of four structured reports at different stages of the project. These presentations will ensure transparency, encourage community participation, and facilitate interaction between villagers, government officials, and development experts. In these presentations not only block level but district and state level officers will be invited. Presentation will be recorded electronically and loaded in website of the village.
1. First Report: Presentation of village after practical training
The first report will be presented in an open meeting of Gram Sabha immediately after the completion of practical training. Under the guidance of subject experts The local youth trained as Change Agents (CAs) will present their initial work, including the village information such as resource maps, social maps, and other relevant data. They will also demonstrate how this information has been documented and being made available in the Gram Panchayat office and website of the village, thereby improving access to village-level information.
2. Second Report: Presentation of Participatory Action Study (PAS)
The second report will be presented in open meeting of Gram Sabha under the chairmanship of a senior government officer. This report will present the findings of the Participatory Action Study (PAS) conducted by the Change Agents and the Project Implementation Team (PIT). The study report will highlight the functioning of village institutions, local development issues, and the status of service delivery systems. The second report will be presented by the subject experts with the support of CAs and PIT members. Decision taken in the meeting will become action plan of Gram Sabha. Action taken on the decisions will be updated in the website of village on regular basis. 
3. Third Report: Presentation of Project Experience and Action Plan
The third report will focus on the overall process and experiences of Project KRANTI, including both facilitating factors and challenges encountered during implementation. This report will be presented in an open meeting of Gram Sabha under the chairmanship of a state level officer in the ministry of rural development, Government of India. During this presentation, the Change Agents will also outline a collective action plan for addressing a specific priority issue identified by the community, demonstrating how local participation can lead to practical solutions.
4. Fourth and Final Report: Evaluation Workshop
The final report will be presented in a workshop organized in the model village, where planners, policy makers, government officials, and development experts will be invited. This workshop will evaluate the overall impact of Project KRANTI, review the participatory processes adopted, and identify best practices and lessons learned. Based on this evaluation, guidance will be sought for designing and implementing KRANTI – Phase II, which aims to replicate successful practices in a village of all 17 RIRDs of the DDU-SIRD, Lucknow Uttar Pradesh.
Through these structured presentations, the project will ensure continuous learning, accountability, and collaboration, while demonstrating a practical model for strengthening people’s governance and Gram Swaraj at the village level.
6.0 Benefits of project:
The implementation of Project KRANTI is expected to generate several social, institutional, and developmental benefits for the village community. The key benefits are as follows:
Live demonstration on PROCESS of strengthening Gram Swaraj: The project will demonstrate how community participation can improve local development and decision-making.
Strengthened local institutions: Regular meetings of the Gram Sabha and committees of the Gram Panchayat will improve transparency, accountability, and effectiveness in local governance.
Digital documentation and transparency: Village-level information, development interventions, and achievements will be documented and made accessible through a dedicated website, ensuring transparency and easy access to information.
Improved financial transparency of SHGs: Accounts and activities of Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and their institutions will be maintained and displayed digitally, promoting accountability and better financial management.
Development of local leadership: Local youth trained as Change Agents (CAs) will actively work for community mobilization and village development, creating a sustainable pool of local leadership.
Strengthened livelihood planning: Job card holders under MGNREGA will prepare and propose annual work plans to improve employment opportunities and local resource development.
Improved financial planning for SHG members: Most SHG members will have Micro Investment Plans (MIPs) and the SHGs have Micro Credit Plans, enabling better financial management, livelihood enhancement, and economic empowerment.
Collaboration between SHGs and Gram Panchayat: The project will encourage institutional collaboration between SHGs and Gram Panchayat, enabling them to work together effectively to address local issues and implement development initiatives.
Greater Community Participation: There will be an increase in community participation in Gram Sabha meetings, especially in activities related to planning, monitoring, and social audit of development programs.
Replication of Best Practices: The project will develop an appropriate model and practical framework that can be replicated in other villages based on the successful experiences and best practices of the model village.
Overall, Project KRANTI will contribute to strengthening participatory governance, improving transparency, empowering local communities, and promoting sustainable rural development, thereby advancing the vision of Gram Swaraj.
