The financial year 2025–26 commenced with a cash balance of ₹120 and no balance in the bank account. Despite severe financial constraints, AIRD continued its commitment to promoting community participation and strengthening the vision of Gram Swaraj in Village Garhi.
Earth Day
On 22 April, Earth Day, a team of AIRD Board of Trustees visited village Garhi without prior intimation to the local community or members of the Self-Help Groups (SHGs). The purpose of the visit was to assess whether the awareness and development initiatives introduced over the previous years had continued independently.
The team observed that, although awareness regarding environmental conservation and community participation had increased, the village had not yet developed the capacity to independently sustain collective development activities. There was little evidence of community-led initiatives to improve local governance or the management of natural resources. The visit reaffirmed AIRD's understanding that continuous mentoring and local leadership are essential for sustaining participatory development.
National Panchayati Raj Day 
A second field visit was undertaken during Panchayati Raj Day celebrations. Prior to the visit, members of the SHGs had been informed that AIRD representatives might not be able to attend and were encouraged to organize the programme independently by inviting elected representatives and villagers to discuss the role of the Gram Panchayat in improving public services and managing local resources.
Although the SHG members assured AIRD over the telephone that the programme would be organized, the visiting team found that no meeting or community celebration had been arranged. No interaction had been planned with the Gram Panchayat or the villagers. The team returned disappointed but viewed the experience as an important learning opportunity. It highlighted that community institutions still require sustained capacity building, motivation, and local leadership before they can independently organize participatory governance activities.
Bank Uncle Day 
A respected supporter and admirer of AIRD's philosophy passed away in France on 18 September 2025. During his final moments, he was accompanied by some of his close friends and the founder of the Development Car concept.
On 25 September, observed as Bank Uncle Day, the AIRD family offered special prayers in his memory and expressed gratitude for his encouragement and moral support towards AIRD's mission of community empowerment. Members prayed for peace to his soul and reaffirmed their commitment to serving humanity.
Development Car Day 
Development Car Day was celebrated at the AIRD office on 3 December to commemorate the birthday of the founder of the Development Car concept. The programme included meditation, discussions on spiritual development, and reflection on AIRD's philosophy of serving the needy as a path to personal and social transformation.
Foundation Day 
AIRD celebrated its Foundation Day on 31 January 2026 with a special meditation programme at Ramakrishna Math, Nirala Nagar, Lucknow. The event provided an opportunity for trustees to reflect on the organization's journey and renew their commitment to establishing a live model of participatory rural development.
This was followed by a meeting of the Board of Trustees on 2 February 2026. After reviewing the organization's progress, the Board concluded that AIRD should continue preserving and refining the KRANTI model while waiting for a suitable institutional partner willing to collaborate in establishing the first live demonstration village. The Board emphasized that meaningful replication should begin only after successfully demonstrating the model in one village.
Support to Regional Institute of Rural Development (RIRD)
Towards the end of February 2026, the Regional Institute of Rural Development (RIRD) requested AIRD's support in analysing data collected from 200 respondents for documenting field realities under the Jal Jeevan Mission.
AIRD successfully completed the assignment and received an honorarium of ₹10,000, which was credited to its bank account. Since the account had remained inactive for several months, the bank deducted applicable maintenance charges before the amount became available. Bank Balance remain only ₹1,677 . 
The year was characterized by reflection rather than expansion. AIRD's field observations clearly indicated that awareness generation alone is insufficient for achieving sustainable community participation. While villagers appreciated the concept of participatory governance, they were unable to independently organize collective activities without continued facilitation.
The Board concluded that future interventions should focus on developing a cadre of trained and locally accountable Change Agents, supported through modest honoraria, to sustain village-level activities. The experience also reinforced AIRD's belief that establishing a single successful live model is essential before scaling the approach to other villages.
Despite limited financial resources, AIRD remained committed to its mission of strengthening Gram Swaraj, promoting transparency in local governance, and developing a practical model of participatory rural development that can be replicated across rural Uttar Pradesh.
