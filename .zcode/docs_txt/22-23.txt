Annual Report 2022–23
Association for Integrated Rural Development (AIRD)
Financial Overview
The financial year 2022–23 commenced with a cash balance of ₹1,350 and a bank balance of ₹16,494.07.
Earth Day Celebration and Environmental Awareness
On 22 April 2022, AIRD celebrated Earth Day in Village Garhi with active participation from members of Self-Help Groups (SHGs), children, and villagers. The day was also observed as the birth anniversary of Prof. M. M. Hoda, widely recognized for his contribution to Appropriate Technology in India.
A slogan-writing competition on environmental conservation was organized for village children. Winners were honoured with gifts, while all 21 participating children received appreciation. To promote environmental awareness, 25 saplings of five different species were distributed among the participants for plantation in the village. Women members of the SHGs played a key role in organizing and conducting the event.
Health Challenge of the Convener
On 10 May 2022, Mrs. Neera Tripathi, Convener of AIRD, was diagnosed with breast cancer. A returnable donation of ₹11,000, which she had earlier provided to facilitate the opening of AIRD's bank account during 2020–21, remained recorded as a liability.
On 18 July 2022, ₹5,000 was refunded to her, reducing the outstanding liability to ₹6,000. Mrs. Tripathi was admitted to the Lucknow Cancer Institute on 25 May 2022, where she underwent successful surgery on 3 June 2022 under the care of Dr. Vivek Garg. Despite this difficult period, AIRD continued its activities with the support of its trustees and volunteers.
Preparations for Project KRANTI
During July 2022, efforts were renewed to mobilize resources for launching Project KRANTI (Key to Reform and Adopt Noble Treatment Initiatives), aimed at developing Village Garhi as a live model of participatory rural development.
During the year, AIRD received ₹33,800 as returnable donations to support preliminary activities for the project. A total of ₹30,360 was spent on field preparations, community meetings, and planning activities.
The Board of Trustees reviewed the field situation and the progress of community mobilization. Although encouraging progress had been made, the Board concluded that additional efforts were required to prepare the community for active participation before formally launching the project.
Celebrations and Spiritual Motivation
On 25 September 2022, the birthday of "Bank Uncle" (Mr. Christopher Baron) was celebrated at the AIRD office. Members of the Board of Trustees expressed their gratitude for his long-standing encouragement of participatory rural development and offered prayers for his good health and well-being.
On 3 December 2022, AIRD celebrated Development Car Day, marking the birthday of the founder of the Development Car concept. The occasion provided an opportunity for the Board of Trustees to review AIRD's vision of integrating spiritual values with community development and service to the needy.
Review of KRANTI Preparations
Following a detailed review on Development Car Day, the Board concluded that Village Garhi still required further awareness-building and community mobilization before implementation of Project KRANTI could begin successfully.
Accordingly, the returnable donations amounting to ₹38,800 (₹33,800 received during the year plus ₹5,000 previously retained) were refunded to the contributors. An outstanding liability of ₹6,000 remained payable at the close of the financial year.
Youth Engagement and Community Mobilization
On 12 January 2023, observed as National Youth Day in honour of Swami Vivekananda, AIRD organized a meeting with village youth to discuss their role in strengthening Gram Sabha participation and village development. The programme was followed by spiritual camps promoting meditation, self-discipline, and community service.
During February 2023, several community meetings were organized in Village Garhi to discuss the responsibilities of youth and women members of SHGs in strengthening Gram Sabha meetings and improving the functioning of the Gram Panchayat.
A community dinner (Maitri Bhoj) was also organized, during which villagers voluntarily contributed food materials and labour. The event strengthened social harmony and encouraged collective participation in village development.
Annual Review
The Annual Meeting of the Board of Trustees was held at the end of the financial year to review the organization's progress.
The Board appreciated the development of the AIRD website, which had begun presenting the organization's vision, activities, and proposed strategy for establishing a live model of participatory rural development in Village Garhi.
Although implementation of Project KRANTI had to be postponed due to the need for further community preparation, the Board expressed confidence that the groundwork laid during the year would strengthen future implementation.

Despite limited financial resources and unforeseen challenges, including the serious illness of the Convener, AIRD continued to strengthen community relationships, promote environmental awareness, encourage youth participation, and advance preparations for its long-term vision of establishing Village Garhi as a live model of participatory rural development and Gram Swaraj.
The Board reaffirmed its commitment to continue working patiently and collaboratively until this vision is translated into reality.
The strategy of AIRD during the year remained focused on community preparedness rather than project implementation. The Board of Trustees believed that establishing a sustainable model of Gram Swaraj requires active ownership by the local community rather than dependence on external funding. Therefore, the year was devoted to strengthening the social foundation necessary for launching Project KRANTI.
The key strategic interventions included:
Continuing environmental awareness and community mobilization through Earth Day celebrations and plantation activities. 
Strengthening relationships with women Self-Help Groups (SHGs), village youth, and community leaders. 
Promoting spiritual values such as selfless service, cooperation, meditation, and social responsibility as motivational tools for community participation. 
Reviewing village readiness through regular field visits and Board meetings before initiating large-scale interventions. 
Developing AIRD's website to improve transparency, document activities, and communicate the vision of developing a live model village. 
Mobilizing voluntary resources while avoiding financial commitments beyond the organization's capacity. 
Analysis of Progress
The year demonstrated that community mobilization is a gradual process requiring continuous interaction, confidence-building, and local leadership.
The environmental programmes successfully encouraged participation of children, women, and families, creating opportunities to discuss broader issues of sustainable development. Community events such as Earth Day, Youth Day, and Maitri Bhoj strengthened social relationships and generated goodwill towards AIRD.
However, field assessments revealed that villagers were not yet sufficiently organized to collectively plan, implement, and monitor village development initiatives. Participation in Gram Sabha, village-level planning, and collective decision-making required further strengthening before Project KRANTI could be launched effectively.
The illness of the Convener also slowed organizational activities during part of the year. Despite this unforeseen challenge, the Board maintained continuity of operations and ensured that the organization's commitments were fulfilled.
The decision to return the voluntary, returnable donations instead of retaining idle funds reflected AIRD's commitment to financial discipline, accountability, and ethical management. The Board considered it more appropriate to postpone implementation than to utilize funds before adequate community preparedness had been achieved.
Key Lessons Learned
Sustainable rural development depends more on community ownership than on financial resources. 
Building trust among villagers requires patience, regular dialogue, and visible transparency. 
Women Self-Help Groups and village youth have significant potential to become local development leaders when provided with appropriate guidance and training. 
Digital documentation and website-based reporting can improve transparency, but village-level information management requires dedicated human resources. 
Project implementation should begin only after sufficient community awareness and institutional readiness have been achieved. 
Based on the experiences of 2022–23, AIRD will continue to strengthen community institutions before launching Project KRANTI. Priority will be given to:
Building a team of local Change Agents (CAs) from village youth and SHGs. 
Increasing participation in Gram Sabha and Gram Panchayat activities. 
Strengthening transparency in village-level institutions through digital documentation. 
Developing an electronic village information bank. 
Establishing partnerships with government departments, universities, NGOs, banks, and corporate organizations. 
Mobilizing adequate financial and technical resources for implementing Project KRANTI in Village Garhi. 
The year 2022–23 was essentially a year of institutional strengthening and community preparation. Although the implementation of Project KRANTI was intentionally deferred, AIRD made important progress in community mobilization, environmental awareness, organizational transparency, and strategic planning. The Board remains committed to establishing Village Garhi as a demonstration model of participatory rural development and Gram Swaraj, ensuring that implementation proceeds only when the community is fully prepared to lead and sustain the process.
