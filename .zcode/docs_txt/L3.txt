Earth Day celebration and tribute to Prof. M. M. Hoda
On 22 April 2021, Earth Day was celebrated as a collective initiative of the Self-Help Groups (SHGs) of Village Garhi under the leadership of Mrs. Sudha. The occasion was also observed as the birthday of Prof. M. M. Hoda, who had been honored by India Development Group UK Ltd., London, as the Father of Appropriate Technology in India.
During the programme, villagers were introduced to the work of Appropriate Technology Development Association (ATDA) and the participatory research conducted under the guidance of Prof. Hoda in various villages of Uttar Pradesh. Discussions focused on the importance of appropriate technologies, community participation, and sustainable rural development. The villagers appreciated the participatory approaches developed through these initiatives and expressed their willingness to work collectively towards demonstrating a practical model of development in Village Garhi.
Strengthening transparency in SHGs
A special meeting was organized to discuss the importance of transparency and accountability in the management of SHGs. Members unanimously agreed that transparent financial management is essential for strengthening community institutions and ensuring their long-term sustainability.
The SHGs decided to resume the sharing of their financial transaction records with AIRD for electronic documentation and analysis. Mrs. Sudha volunteered to coordinate the collection and dissemination of information from SHGs. Despite her sincere efforts, transaction records could be collected from only five SHGs.
This experience once again highlighted the need for creating a dedicated and remunerated position of Community Information Facilitator within the proposed village development project to ensure regular collection, documentation, and dissemination of information.
Bank Uncle Day
On 25 September 2021, members of the SHGs celebrated “Bank Uncle Day” in Village Garhi. The event was organized to honor the philosophy of finding happiness through service to the needy and promoting mutual support within the community.
The celebration reinforced the values of compassion, cooperation, and community solidarity, which are central to AIRD's approach to development.
Foundation Day of AIRD
The Foundation Day of AIRD was celebrated on 3 December 2021. The programme began with a session of deep meditation at Ramakrishna Math, Lucknow, followed by a special prayer by members of the Board of Trustees.
The prayer was dedicated to the individual who introduced the concept of the Development Car, whose birthday falls on the same date. Although she resides in London and prefers to remain anonymous, her inspiration continues to guide the philosophy and activities of AIRD.
Development of AIRD Website
As directed by the Board of Trustees, efforts continued to develop AIRD's official website as a platform for sharing information on activities, achievements, research findings, and opportunities for collaboration.
The website was conceived as a tool to connect local communities with universities, development organizations, researchers, volunteers, and institutions interested in rural development and participatory governance.
Through the website, AIRD seeks to invite individuals and organizations to join hands with villagers and contribute towards the realization of Gram Swaraj through collective action.
Swami Vivekananda Jayanti
Members of the Board of Trustees participated in the celebration of Swami Vivekananda Jayanti at Ramakrishna Math. The programme inspired participants to reflect on the values of selfless service, leadership, and nation-building advocated by Swami Vivekananda.
The celebration was followed by a meeting at the AIRD office, where discussions focused on strengthening outreach efforts and using the website as a platform for inviting collaboration from people and institutions interested in rural transformation.
Workshop on People's Governance
A five-day workshop was organized at the AIRD office from 26 January to 30 January 2022 to discuss the concept of people's governance and the vision of Gram Swaraj. Participants examined the challenges faced by rural institutions and explored practical approaches for strengthening participation, transparency, accountability, and community ownership in local governance.
The workshop also provided an opportunity to review existing development processes and identify innovative methods for improving governance at the Gram Panchayat level.
Proposal for establishing an Appropriate Ashram
On 2 February 2022, a special meeting of SHG members was organized in Village Garhi to discuss the possibility of establishing an Appropriate Ashram as a centre for integrated community development.
The proposed Ashram was envisioned as a platform for promoting spiritual growth, leadership development, environmental awareness, participatory planning, livelihood promotion, and community education.
During the meeting, Mr. Jitendra Shukla generously expressed his willingness to make a small parcel of land near the village nursery available for initiating the development of the proposed Ashram. The proposal was welcomed by community members and considered a significant step towards creating a permanent community learning centre.
Formation of Project Implementation Team (PIT)
To strengthen community participation and local ownership, a Project Implementation Team (PIT) comprising 12 active leaders of Self-Help Groups was constituted on 2 February 2022.
The PIT was entrusted with supporting community mobilization, identifying local issues, facilitating village-level meetings, and assisting in the formulation and implementation of development initiatives.
Conceptualization of KRANTI Project
On 4 March 2022, a team of development practitioners and subject experts visited Village Garhi to work with local residents on conceptualizing the KRANTI (Key to Reform and Adopt Noble Treatment Initiatives) Project.
The visit provided an opportunity for community members, SHG leaders, and experts to jointly discuss local development challenges and identify strategies for transforming Garhi into a live model of participatory development and Gram Swaraj.
As a symbol of this collaborative effort, a project banner and vision statement were prepared jointly by villagers and experts, reflecting their shared commitment to creating a demonstration village based on participation, transparency, accountability, sustainability, and community empowerment.
The initiative marked an important milestone in AIRD's journey towards developing and demonstrating a practical model of rural development that could eventually be replicated in other villages.

Annual Meeting of the Board of Trustees (BoT) – March 2022
On 30 March 2022, the Annual Meeting of the Board of Trustees (BoT) was held at the registered office of AIRD. The meeting reviewed the progress made during the year and discussed future strategies for strengthening the organization's efforts to establish Village Garhi as a live model of participatory development and Gram Swaraj.
The members appreciated the development of the AIRD website and provided valuable suggestions for improving its content, presentation, and usefulness as a platform for sharing information and promoting collaboration. The Board also expressed satisfaction that AIRD had successfully obtained registration with NITI Aayog and had secured approval under Sections 12A and 80G of the Income Tax Act, thereby strengthening the institutional credibility of the organization.
Review of SHG Information System
The Board reviewed the efforts made to establish a computerized information system for documenting SHG transactions and activities. Members were not surprised to learn that the information collection system could not be sustained solely through voluntary efforts because of time constraints faced by community members.
The Board reiterated its earlier recommendation that information collection and documentation should be treated as a dedicated paid responsibility within future projects to ensure regularity, quality, and sustainability.
Proposal for Participatory Study and Action Research
The Board recommended initiating a Participatory Study on Village Governance Systems, followed by a Participatory Action Research (PAR) programme aimed at identifying practical measures for improving local governance and community participation.
The proposed study would document:
Strengths and weaknesses of Self-Help Groups (SHGs); 
Factors facilitating and constraining their effectiveness; 
Effectiveness of MGNREGA in ensuring job cards for all eligible households and providing up to 100 days of employment; 
Community perceptions regarding the functioning of elected representatives and Gram Sabha institutions; 
Status of participation, transparency, accountability, and service delivery at the village level. 
The findings would serve as baseline information and be presented through AIRD's website to provide a clear picture of village governance prior to the implementation of development interventions.
Vision for KRANTI
The Board recommended that the proposed KRANTI (Key to Reform and Adopt Noble Treatment Initiatives) project should focus on strengthening local governance institutions and increasing community participation in development processes.
The project should seek to:
Facilitate planning for 100 days of employment for all willing job card holders under MGNREGA. 
Strengthen the functioning of all seven statutory committees of the Gram Panchayat. 
Promote greater participation of women in decision-making and community governance. 
Improve management of local resources and public services. 
Enhance transparency, accountability, and social responsibility at the village level. 
Community-Led Development Planning
The Board emphasized that local development priorities should be identified directly by community members. Based on the issues identified, villagers should be organized into thematic working groups.
These groups would be supported to:
Analyze local problems and opportunities; 
Interact with relevant government departments and technical experts; 
Formulate practical development projects; 
Seek partnerships with government agencies, banks, and corporate institutions for implementation. 
This approach would strengthen local ownership and encourage collaborative problem-solving.
Strengthening SHGs and Community Documentation
The Board further recommended providing support to all 12 Self-Help Groups of Village Garhi and their village-level federation for maintaining regular records of:
Financial transactions, 
Meetings, 
Social initiatives, 
Community development activities. 
Community feedback regarding KRANTI activities should also be documented electronically and periodically shared through the AIRD website to promote transparency and encourage wider participation.
Formation of a Project Team
Under KRANTI, a multidisciplinary Project Team consisting of subject experts and local stakeholders was proposed. The team would work closely with:
Members of the SHG Federation, 
Elected representatives of the Gram Panchayat, 
Local youth, 
Community leaders. 
The objective would be to strengthen the community's capacity to plan, implement, monitor, and evaluate development projects and to conduct social audits of village-level interventions.
Development of Change Agents
Recognizing the importance of local leadership, the Board recommended that SHGs identify a group of motivated village youth who possess the time, energy, and commitment required to serve their community.
These youth would be trained as Change Agents (CAs) through practical, field-based training programmes covering:
Participatory planning, 
Rural development programmes, 
Community mobilization, 
Project management, 
Monitoring and evaluation, 
Social audit, 
Digital documentation, 
Resource management. 
The long-term objective would be to create a cadre of trained local leaders capable of facilitating development initiatives independently.
Establishment of an E-Information Bank
As part of the training process, the Board proposed the establishment of an E-Information Bank in Village Garhi. The information bank would be linked to the AIRD website and serve as a repository of information related to:
Village resources, 
Development programmes, 
Community institutions, 
Employment opportunities, 
Government schemes, 
Project implementation and outcomes. 
Local youth would be trained to maintain and update this information system both during and after the project period.
Capacity Building for Community Empowerment
The Board emphasized that practical training should empower community members to:
Identify local development issues; 
Conduct constructive dialogue with government officials; 
Formulate project proposals; 
Mobilize resources; 
Implement development initiatives; 
Monitor progress; 
Conduct social audits and public reviews. 
Such capacity-building efforts would strengthen community ownership and promote long-term sustainability.
Project review and certification
At the completion of the KRANTI project period, the Board proposed organizing a workshop in the model village to review achievements, challenges, lessons learned, and the effectiveness of the participatory processes adopted.
Development practitioners, researchers, government representatives, and subject experts would be invited to study and evaluate the model. Youth who successfully complete the training programme and demonstrate competence in community facilitation would be recognized as Certified Change Agents (CAs) and encouraged to participate in future phases of the initiative.
The meeting concluded with a collective reaffirmation of AIRD's commitment to promoting Gram Swaraj, participatory governance, transparency, accountability, and sustainable rural development. Members offered a special prayer seeking guidance and success in the organization's mission to establish Village Garhi as a live demonstration model of community-led development and people's governance.
